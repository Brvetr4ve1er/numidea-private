# Market and buyers

What buyers in each market expect, and where Numidea Labs stands against it. This summarises the studio's private research of October 2026: a scored comparison of web sellers in Algeria, France and the Gulf, a channel study, and the site audit. **It is for planning only.** Never name another studio or seller in public, never compare the studio with one, and never turn a finding here into a claim ("the only studio that…").

## Where the studio stands

- **Scores, out of 42:** Algeria 26 (median 24.5; the best seller scored 32), France 24 (median 27; best 35), the Gulf about 23, an estimate (median 27; best 34).
- **It leads on risk reversal and design.** The Aperçu is the strongest risk reversal in all three markets: none of the 12 Algerian sellers scored builds anything before payment. Native right-to-left Arabic is rare in Algeria, absent among the global benchmarks and patchy in the Gulf. Its prices are published, beside a market table that cites its sources.
- **It scores 0 on proof from others in every market.** What loses buyers is what they find when they check: a `github.io` address, a personal Gmail, no registration number, no one else vouching for the work.
- **Social media is where a buyer checks a studio, not where the deal closes.** Studio accounts in all three markets are small: the largest Algerian studio page found has about 20,700 Facebook followers, most have a few hundred, and the Gulf studios' TikTok accounts have single- or double-digit followings. The sellers that score best win through search, directories and WhatsApp.

## Algeria

**The buyer** is the owner or manager of a company, ideally a registered one (the Aperçu requires it).

**What they expect:**
- WhatsApp first, with a pre-filled message and a reply promise (one seller promises 4 working hours);
- signs of a real business: an address, RC and NIF numbers, promises about who owns what;
- a Facebook page: nearly every seller links one, and Facebook and Instagram are the main ad platforms there;
- a price they can compare: visible packs at 20,000–60,000 DA, and classified ads at 10,000–50,000 DA (unverified).

**Where they look:** French dominates search; Arabic queries return few Algerian agencies. No web agency ranks for Bordj Bou Arréridj in French or Arabic, and a search for "Numidea Labs" returned nothing, with similarly named businesses outranking it. Audience (DataReportal 2026): Facebook 27.5 M, Instagram 13.5 M, TikTok 24.8 M adults, YouTube 25.4 M, LinkedIn 5.5 M members; 25–34 is the largest Facebook age group. On language, a 2024 study of Algerian live sales found French used for prestige and Algerian Arabic for comprehension, with too much code-switching putting some viewers off: one language per post.

**What they doubt:** "Why pay 90,000 DA when packs cost 20,000–35,000?"

**Where the studio stands:**
- Essentiel (90,000–160,000 DA) sits above the Algerian showcase range the site itself cites (50,000–100,000 DA) and far above the visible packs; Studio and Moteur sit inside the Algerian ranges. Answer with what Essentiel includes, line by line, never with a comparison.
- WhatsApp and a reply within 24 working hours exist; the number is personal, and the WhatsApp call to action waits for a dedicated one (Gate B).
- No address, RC or NIF is published.
- Arabic, done natively, and the studio's own town are real advantages; say them plainly.

## France

**The buyer** is the head (dirigeant) of a small or medium firm (TPE, PME).

**What they check before paying a studio abroad:**
- a company they can verify: the legal notice (mentions légales, mandatory in France), a SIREN-type registration;
- an invoice their accountant accepts;
- a contract under a known law;
- protection after payment, and who owns what;
- a named contact;
- reviews, with counts.

**Where they look:** offshore studios are found through directories and freelance marketplaces; French price searches are owned by directories, marketplaces and sellers with very large page counts. Small firms are on social networks at 64% (Facebook 48%, LinkedIn 47%, Instagram 47%), and firms three years old or more at higher rates (Facebook 72%, LinkedIn 69%, Instagram 64%): the established firms are the buyers, and LinkedIn is the first channel.

**What they doubt:** "Can I trust a studio abroad?" They are lost on proof, not on price.

**Where the studio stands:**
- Every package sits below the typical French ranges at the official rate (say it only with the fixed sources line in `business.md`, which names no studio), and Algeria shares Paris time all winter.
- The Aperçu answers the "paying for a promise" fear, from Gate B.
- No legal notice, no registration, a personal Gmail and a `github.io` address: exactly what a French buyer checks first. The France push waits for Gate A.
- One French project is on the site, and its client's own domain serves a different site: never present it as live there.
- Whether the Loi Toubon applies to organic posts aimed at France is a question for the lawyer; until then, French-only screens.

## The Gulf

**The buyer** is an SME manager in Dubai or Riyadh.

**What they expect:**
- a price in AED or SAR;
- WhatsApp on a local number, with a reply time;
- a legal identity;
- openness about working from abroad;
- speed, counted in days;
- Arabic that is written, not translated.

**Where they look:** English agency searches return the big directories first; price searches return guides; Arabic city searches reward city pages. In the UAE many small sellers are still English-only; in Saudi Arabia Arabic is the default. Audience (DataReportal 2026): Saudi Arabia TikTok 38.6 M adults, Instagram 18.2 M, Snapchat 25.3 M, LinkedIn 12.0 M members; UAE TikTok 12.5 M, Instagram 8.05 M, Facebook 9.70 M, LinkedIn 10.0 M.

**What they doubt:** "Is this real, and is it for us?"

**Where the studio stands:**
- No AED or SAR price exists, so Gulf pieces show none; dollars only if asked, in a caption or a message.
- No local number, and none is pretended: every piece says the studio is based in Algeria (UTC+1, 2 h behind Riyadh, 3 h behind Dubai).
- Native Arabic is the strongest card here: the site mirrors to right to left, and Arabic is the founder's mother tongue. The site's Arabic still awaits a native review.
- English and Arabic links shared on WhatsApp unfurl a French preview card, because no English or Arabic page of their own exists yet (owner decision pending).

## The biggest gaps

The research names three things that matter most: **a business a buyer can check, proof from other people, and written terms for after the yes.** In its ranked order:

1. **Terms after the yes.** Nothing says what the buyer pays first and how, whether tax is added to a dinar price, who owns the site and the domain, what hosting costs each year, how many revision rounds there are, or what the included support covers. The site promises « Zéro surprise » and « sans frais cachés » while none of this is written. Marketing says nothing about any of it until the owner decides (Gate D).
2. **A verifiable business.** No legal identity, no legal notice, no registration number, no checkable address, no own domain: a personal Gmail and a `github.io` address. The buyers the Aperçu requires to be « immatriculées » meet a studio that is not visibly registered itself (Gate A).
3. **Proof from others.** No quotes, no reviews, no consented case study; no client has been asked for permission; the founder's CV on the same page lists two of the four "client" sites as dated jobs; no client domain serves the delivered build (Gate C).
4. Who the buyer deals with: an unconfirmed team of four against a personal page that says "agency of one", and a monogram where a photo would be.
5. Gulf prices in AED and SAR, and a "we work from Algeria" block.
6. Business profiles: Google Business Profile, a Facebook Page, Instagram and LinkedIn pages of the studio's own.
7. Client sites on their own domains, with a credit to the studio.
8. An honest proof line, and one case shown in full.
9. A light maintenance plan (the research puts the norm near 72 € a month, below the studio's 120–400 €) and a "from" price per package.
10. Directories.
11. A few pages that can rank: a Bordj Bou Arréridj page, price guides, English and Arabic pages.
12. A phone number buyers can call, and checkable hours for « 24 h ouvrées ».
13. Priced add-ons for online shops, and possibly a faster small offer.

## What the research says not to copy

Unsourced counters; self-awarded rankings; permanent discounts or invented scarcity; concepts shown as client work; trackers and widgets; a lead database that breaks the "nothing is stored" promise; lock-in language; 100% up front; content in bulk; pretending to be local; comparisons without a source or naming a competitor; letting proof decay.

## What the self-audit calls weak

- Ten services blur the focus for a small studio; three of the five main cards sell to a different buyer than the one a website needs (the owner chose to keep them, 2026-10-26).
- The case studies state no problem and no result (by design: no result can be backed yet).
- Prices open in dinars for English and Arabic visitors unless the link sets `?cur=`.
- The home page is long: the first price came 12.7 phone screens down in the October audit (the owner kept the order).
